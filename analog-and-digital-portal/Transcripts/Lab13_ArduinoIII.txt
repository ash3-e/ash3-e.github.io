Lab 13: Arduino III (Power Supplies, Driving Large Currents, PID Temperature Regulation)
Overview
Third Arduino lab combining everything from the BJT, op-amp, and Arduino sections of the course. The student first learns the difference between a floating, positive-rail-grounded, or negative-rail-grounded laboratory DC supply (and where Earth ground enters the circuit when GND is connected to one of the supply rails), then uses a 2N3725 power BJT (controlled by Arduino digital pin 13 through a 180 Ω base resistor) to switch a much larger load current (two LEDs in parallel through 330 Ω resistors, powered from a 10 V floating supply) than the Arduino can drive on its own (40 mA per pin), uses PWM on a ~ pin to fade the LEDs, then reads a K-type thermocouple (41 µV/°C) through a MAX6675 amplifier IC and uses the same transistor circuit to drive a 130 Ω power resistor (the heater) under closed-loop PID control. The PID loop multiplies the present error e = SP − PV by gains K_P, K_I, K_D and writes the sum to the PWM output; the student tunes K_P, K_I, K_D from the Serial Monitor and watches the temperature regulate around the setpoint. This transcript is a page-specific reconstruction for preview and search.

Problem coverage
The set is centered on actuating real-world loads from a microcontroller and on a complete closed-loop control example. The pages clearly include the lab-supply tour (a single-output supply has +, −, and GND terminals, with the +/− floating relative to GND unless a jumper wires GND to one of them, giving either a positive or negative ground-referenced supply), the rationale for using a transistor switch instead of driving the load directly (Arduino can supply only ~50 mA at 5 V), the 2N3725 pin-out and the wiring V_S = 10 V → 330 Ω → LED → collector, base via 180 Ω from Arduino digital pin 13 (or PWM pin 9), emitter to ground, the LED-current measurement V_R = 6.52 V → I = 6.52/330 ≈ 19.8 mA per LED so a 0.05 A bus can drive about 2 LEDs from the Arduino directly while the transistor-switched circuit can drive many more (limited by the supply and the transistor rating), the PWM-controlled fading where the transistor moves between longer/shorter active intervals giving the illusion of continuously dimming light, the K-type thermocouple physics (junction of nickel-chromium / nickel-aluminum producing 41 µV/°C, range −200 °C to 1250 °C), the MAX6675 amplifier IC that handles the µV-level signal and reports temperature digitally, and the PID temperature-control circuit (180 Ω base resistor, 130 Ω heater, PWM on pin 11, thermocouple clipped to the heater) plus the PID algorithm where output = K_P·e + K_I·∫e dt + K_D·de/dt and the Serial Monitor commands p / i / d set the gains live (PID strip-chart shows SP as | and PV as − with + when they coincide).

Key equations

$$I_{LED}=\frac{V_{R_{330}}}{330\ \Omega}\quad\text{(LED current from voltage drop)}$$

$$\text{Example: }I=\frac{6.52\ \text{V}}{330\ \Omega}\approx 19.8\ \text{mA}\ (\text{per LED branch})$$

$$\text{Arduino limit per pin: }I_{pin}\le 40\ \text{mA};\ \text{per V_{CC}/GND: }I\le 200\ \text{mA}$$

$$\text{Max LEDs from a single I/O pin: }N=\left\lfloor\frac{40\ \text{mA}}{I_{LED}}\right\rfloor\approx 2\ \text{LEDs}$$

$$I_{C}=\beta I_{B}\quad\text{(transistor amplifies arduino base current)}$$

$$I_{B}=\frac{V_{out,\text{Arduino}}-V_{BE}}{R_{base}}=\frac{5-0.6}{180}\approx 24\ \text{mA}$$

$$I_{C,\max}=\beta I_{B}\quad\text{(load current achievable through collector)}$$

$$\bar V_{load}=D\cdot V_{S}\quad\text{(PWM with duty }D\text{ and supply }V_{S}\text{ across load)}$$

$$\text{Thermocouple sensitivity: }\frac{dV}{dT}=41\ \mu\text{V/}^{\circ}\text{C (K-type)}$$

$$\Delta V=41\ \mu\text{V/K}\cdot\Delta T\quad\text{(small voltage from large temperature change)}$$

$$T\in[-200,+1250]\ ^{\circ}\text{C}\quad\text{(K-type usable range)}$$

$$\text{PID error: }e(t)=\text{SP}-\text{PV}(t)$$

$$\text{PID output: }CO(t)=K_{P}\,e(t)+K_{I}\!\int_{0}^{t}\!\!e(\tau)\,d\tau+K_{D}\,\frac{de}{dt}$$

$$\text{Discrete: }CO_{n}=K_{P}\,e_{n}+K_{I}\Delta t\!\!\sum_{k=0}^{n}e_{k}+\frac{K_{D}}{\Delta t}(e_{n}-e_{n-1})$$

$$\text{Heater dissipation: }P_{heater}=\frac{V_{S}^{2}}{R_{heater}}\cdot D=\frac{10^{2}}{130}\,D\approx 0.77\,D\ \text{W}$$

$$\text{Heater time constant: }\tau_{th}=\frac{C_{th}}{1/R_{th}}\quad\text{(thermal RC of resistor)}$$

$$\text{Steady-state: }CO_{ss}=\frac{1}{K_{I}}\cdot 0\Rightarrow CO_{ss}=K_{I}\!\int e\,dt\ \text{drives }e_{ss}\to 0$$

$$\text{P-only steady-state error: }e_{ss}\propto\frac{1}{K_{P}\cdot G_{plant}}\quad\text{(non-zero residual)}$$

$$\text{Tuning starting points: PI most often used; PD rare; full PID for fast transient + zero offset}$$

$$\text{Serial Monitor commands: pxx.xx, ixx.xx, dxx.xx set K_{P}, K_{I}, K_{D};\ <space>\ starts loop}$$

$$f_{PWM}\approx 490\ \text{Hz},\ T_{PWM}\approx 2\ \text{ms}\ll\tau_{th}\Rightarrow\text{heater sees average}\ \bar V_{load}$$

How to use this page
Read each displayed relation as solving one of four jobs: choosing whether the Arduino can drive a load directly (≤ 40 mA) or whether a transistor switch is required, computing the average power delivered to a PWM-driven load when the load's thermal/electrical time constant is much longer than the PWM period, reading a millivolt-level thermocouple voltage through a sensor-amplifier IC, or assembling P / I / D terms into a closed-loop control output that drives the error to zero. That is the logic tying the handwritten algebra together.
