Lab 7: Field-Effect Transistor (FET)
Overview
Lab on the n-channel JFET (2N5485) used to extract the device's pinch-off voltage V_P, the saturation drain current I_DSS at V_GS = 0, and the I_D vs V_GS characteristic; then to build a common-source amplifier and a constant-current source. The student measures V_P at the gate voltage where I_D drops to zero, records I_DSS at V_GS = 0, plots I_D versus V_GS over the active range, fits the square-law model I_D = K(V_GS − V_P)² to recover K, and exploits the gate's reverse bias (no DC current → input impedance up to 10¹⁴ Ω). The common-source amplifier with an R_S source resistor and an R_D drain resistor yields a self-biased operating point; the constant-current source ties V_GS = 0 (gate to source) so I_D is fixed at I_DSS independent of the load up to the onset of pinch-off. Problem coverage
The set is centered on JFET dc characterisation and bias-point design. The pages clearly include the n-channel JFET conventions (drain held more positive than source, gate biased negative relative to source, no gate DC current), the pinch-off voltage definition V_P as the V_GS at which I_D ≈ 0 (typically around −3 V), the maximum saturation current I_DSS measured with V_GS = 0 (gate tied to source) in the saturation region, the I_D vs V_GS table swept from V_P to 0 V and the square-law fit I_D = K(V_GS − V_P)² (linearised by √I_D = √K(V_GS − V_P) with x-intercept V_P and slope √K), the ohmic-region resistance R_DS = V_DS/I_D for V_DS ≤ |V_P| at fixed V_GS, the common-source amplifier where the source resistor R_S provides self-bias (V_GS = −I_D R_S) and the drain resistor R_D sets the gain G = −g_m R_D with g_m = 2K(V_GS − V_P), the constant-current source where V_GS = 0 fixes I_D = I_DSS up to the load that pulls V_DS below V_DS,sat ≈ |V_P|, and the maximum-load condition V_RL,max = V_S − V_DS,sat giving R_L,max = V_RL,max/I_DSS.

Key equations

$$I_{D}=K(V_{GS}-V_{P})^{2}\quad\text{(JFET square-law in saturation)}$$

$$\sqrt{I_{D}}=\sqrt{K}\,(V_{GS}-V_{P})\quad\text{(linearised form)}$$

$$V_{P}\approx -3\ \text{V (typical 2N5485)},\quad I_{DSS}=I_{D}|_{V_{GS}=0}$$

$$g_{m}=\frac{dI_{D}}{dV_{GS}}=2K(V_{GS}-V_{P})\quad\text{(transconductance)}$$

$$g_{m,max}=2\sqrt{K\,I_{DSS}}\quad\text{(at }V_{GS}=0\text{)}$$

$$\text{Self-bias: }V_{GS}=-I_{D}R_{S}\quad\text{(source resistor pulls gate negative)}$$

$$\text{Operating point from intersection of }I_{D}=K(V_{GS}-V_{P})^{2}\text{ and }V_{GS}=-I_{D}R_{S}$$

$$\text{Common-source gain: }G=-g_{m}R_{D}$$

$$\text{Ohmic region: }0\le V_{DS}\le |V_{P}|\quad\text{(at fixed }V_{GS}\text{)}$$

$$R_{DS}=\frac{V_{DS}}{I_{D}}\bigg|_{\text{ohmic}}\quad\text{(linear resistance estimate)}$$

$$R_{DS,on}=\frac{1}{2K(V_{GS}-V_{P})}\quad\text{(small-signal on-resistance)}$$

$$\text{Constant-current source: }V_{GS}=0\Rightarrow I_{D}=I_{DSS}\ \text{(independent of }V_{DS}\text{ in saturation)}$$

$$V_{DS,sat}\approx |V_{P}|\quad\text{(boundary between ohmic and saturation)}$$

$$V_{RL,max}=V_{S}-V_{DS,sat}\quad\text{(headroom for the load)}$$

$$R_{L,max}=\frac{V_{RL,max}}{I_{DSS}}\quad\text{(largest load before current droops)}$$

$$Z_{in}^{\text{gate}}\sim 10^{14}\ \Omega\quad\text{(gate is reverse-biased pn junction)}$$

$$I_{G}\approx 0\quad\text{(gate leakage only)}$$

$$\Delta V_{GS}=-\Delta I_{D}\,R_{S}\quad\text{(small-signal source-degeneration)}$$

$$|G_{degen}|=\frac{R_{D}}{R_{S}+1/g_{m}}\quad\text{(degenerated gain)}$$

$$P_{trans}=V_{DS}\cdot I_{D}\quad\text{(JFET power dissipation)}$$

How to use this page
Read each displayed relation as solving one of four jobs: extracting V_P and I_DSS from the I-V data and fitting K through the square-law, deciding whether the JFET is in ohmic or saturation operation by comparing V_DS to |V_P|, designing a self-biased common-source stage by intersecting the device curve with the load line V_GS = −I_D R_S, or running the device as a constant-current source by tying V_GS = 0 and reading I_DSS directly. That is the logic tying the handwritten algebra together.
