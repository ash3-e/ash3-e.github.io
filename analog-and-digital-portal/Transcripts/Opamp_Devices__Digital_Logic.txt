Op-amp Devices and Digital Logic
Overview
Single-page lecture notes that walk from op-amp-based building blocks (open-loop comparator, Schmitt trigger with hysteresis, relaxation oscillator, 555-timer integrated circuit) into the elementary digital logic primitives (NOT, AND, NAND, OR, NOR, XOR with truth tables), then a bistable NAND-NAND flip-flop "memory" that is extended into a gated D-latch and chained into shift-register-style memory, then a review of field-effect transistors (JFET, depletion-mode MOSFET, enhancement-mode E-MOSFET, P-mos), then how to actually realise logic gates from a bipolar transistor and from a CMOS pair, and finally how to combine those gates into a half-adder, a full-adder stage, and a ripple-carry 4-bit adder. The page emphasises how op-amp gain rolls off above the unity-gain bandwidth (~10⁵–10⁶ Hz), why a Schmitt trigger reduces switching jitter on noisy signals, how positive feedback turns the same comparator into a free-running RC oscillator, why CMOS draws essentially no static current (and so dissipates much less power than a single-transistor NOT gate with a pull-up resistor), and how 4 one-bit adders (≈ 20 logic gates ≈ 80 transistors) combine into a 4-bit adder — the page's punchline about "emergent computational complexity." This transcript is a page-specific reconstruction for preview and search.

Problem coverage
The set is centred on signed-output op-amp circuits, the elementary Boolean primitives, the transistor-level realisation of those primitives, and binary arithmetic. The page clearly includes the open-loop transfer V_out = A_OL(V_in⁺ − V_in⁻) with A_OL ≫ 1 and a roll-off frequency around the unity-gain point, the comparator output that latches to ±V_S as soon as V_in crosses zero, the Schmitt trigger with the symmetric hysteretic thresholds V_b = ±V_S R₁/(R₁+R₂) ≈ ±1 V using R₁ = 90 kΩ and R₂ = 10 kΩ, the relaxation oscillator built from the same Schmitt feedback wrapped around an R_F C charging branch (V_out = +V_S → cap charges → V_in⁻ crosses V_in⁺ → flip → cap discharges → flip back), the 555-timer block diagram with its 2/3 V_S and 1/3 V_S thresholds and its internal flip-flop with Q and Q̄ outputs (used as oscillators and timers), the digital-logic mapping 0 ↔ 0 V (range 0–3 V) and 1 ↔ +5 V (range 3–5 V), and full truth tables for NOT, AND, NAND, OR, NOR, XOR. It then covers the cross-coupled NAND flip-flop bistable memory whose state holds when both inputs are HIGH and is set or reset by pulling S or R low, and extends that into a D-latch built by tethering two more NAND gates so a single Data line D plus an Enable line E drive Q and Q̄ — chained D-latches with their enables wired in series form a shift register / connected array of memory elements. The FET review covers the n-channel JFET (gate, source, drain, depletion region, undoped channel) with the I_d–V_ds family at V_gs = 0, −1 V, −2 V, V_gs < −V_p showing an active/linear region and a saturation (switch) region where I_d is independent of V_ds, the depletion-mode MOSFET (insulating oxide allows V_gs > 0, which pulls minority carriers in the p-region toward the gate and enhances the n-channel; bulk/body electrode usually tied to source), the enhancement-mode N-channel E-MOSFET (off at V_gs = 0, conducts only for V_gs > 0, body tied to reverse-bias the P-N junction; one E-MOSFET with a pull-up resistor is a NOT gate), and the P-mos / JFET on/off conventions (P-mos FET on for V_gs = 0, off for V_gs > 0). The "How to make Logic Gates" section gives the single-bipolar-transistor NOT gate (V_in = 0 → off → V_out ≈ 5 V; V_in = 5 V → driven into saturation by R₁ and R₂ → V_CE small → V_out ≈ 0) and the CMOS inverter (P-mos on top, n-mos on bottom: V_in = +5 V flips P-mos off and n-mos on, pulling V_out to ground; V_in = 0 V flips P-mos on and n-mos off, pulling V_out to +V_S), with the key observation that no DC path exists from +V_S to GND in either steady state, so CMOS dissipates much less static power than a transistor + resistor NOT gate. A two-input CMOS gate is then drawn with two stacked P-mos (Q₁, Q₂) and two stacked n-mos (Q₃, Q₄), with A controlling Q₂ and Q₃ and B controlling Q₁ and Q₄, using the conventions n-mos: g=1 on (V_gs = V_d − V_g), g=0 off and P-mos: g=0 on (V_gs = V_s − V_g), g=1 off. Finally the "Adding Bits" section gives the base-10 ↔ base-2 mapping for 0–7, the worked example 10101₂ = 1·2⁴ + 0·2³ + 1·2² + 0·2¹ + 1·2⁰ = 21, a binary addition example 3 + 2 = 5 (011 + 010 = 101 with a carry), the half-adder sub-unit S = A ⊕ B and C = A·B with A + B = CS, the full-adder stage with A, B, C_in inputs and S, C_out outputs (two XORs, two ANDs, one OR — "add A+B then add C_in at each step and check to see if we need to carry another one to the next bit"), the 4-bit operands A = A₈A₄A₂A₁ and B = B₈B₄B₂B₁ summing to C_8,out S₈S₄S₂S₁, the ripple-carry stack of four full-adder blocks, the boxed 4-bit Adder symbol with C_in/C_out, and the gate/transistor budget: 1 four-bit adder = 4 one-bit adders = 4 × 5 logic gates = 4 × 5 × 4 transistors = 80 transistors — flagged as emergent computational complexity, with two 4-bit adders chainable to add wider numbers.

Key equations

$$V_{out}=A_{OL}(V_{in}^{+}-V_{in}^{-}),\qquad A_{OL}\gg 1$$

$$\text{Golden Rule (with feedback): }V_{in}^{+}=V_{in}^{-},\qquad I_{1}=I_{2}\quad\text{(Input draws no current)}$$

$$\text{Comparator: }V_{out}=\begin{cases}+V_{S} & V_{in}^{+}>V_{in}^{-}\\ -V_{S} & V_{in}^{+}<V_{in}^{-}\end{cases}$$

$$V_{b}=\pm V_{S}\,\frac{R_{1}}{R_{1}+R_{2}}=\pm 1\ \text{V}\ \text{(Schmitt thresholds, }R_{1}=90\,\text{k}\Omega,R_{2}=10\,\text{k}\Omega,V_{S}=1.0\ \text{V)}$$

$$\text{Relaxation oscillator: }V_{C}(t)=V_{S}+(V_{C}(0)-V_{S})\,e^{-t/\tau},\ \ \tau=R_{F}C$$

$$T_{osc}=2R_{F}C\,\ln\!\left[\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}\right],\quad \beta=\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}+R_{2}}$$

$$\text{555 thresholds: }\tfrac{2}{3}V_{S}\ \text{(upper)},\ \tfrac{1}{3}V_{S}\ \text{(lower)}\ \text{— used as oscillators and timers}$$

$$\text{Logic levels: }0\leftrightarrow 0\text{–}3\ \text{V},\ \ 1\leftrightarrow 3\text{–}5\ \text{V}$$

$$\text{NOT}(A)=\bar A:\ 0\!\to\!1,\ 1\!\to\!0$$

$$\text{AND}(A,B)=A\!\cdot\!B:\ (0,0)\!\to\!0,\ (1,0)\!\to\!0,\ (0,1)\!\to\!0,\ (1,1)\!\to\!1$$

$$\text{NAND}(A,B)=\overline{A\!\cdot\!B}:\ (0,0)\!\to\!1,\ (1,0)\!\to\!1,\ (0,1)\!\to\!1,\ (1,1)\!\to\!0$$

$$\text{OR}(A,B)=A+B:\ (0,0)\!\to\!0,\ (1,0)\!\to\!1,\ (0,1)\!\to\!1,\ (1,1)\!\to\!1$$

$$\text{NOR}(A,B)=\overline{A+B}:\ (0,0)\!\to\!1,\ (1,0)\!\to\!0,\ (0,1)\!\to\!0,\ (1,1)\!\to\!0$$

$$\text{XOR}(A,B)=A\oplus B:\ (0,0)\!\to\!0,\ (1,0)\!\to\!1,\ (0,1)\!\to\!1,\ (1,1)\!\to\!0$$

$$\text{NAND flip-flop holds state when both inputs are HIGH; }Q\ \text{and }\bar Q\ \text{are complementary}$$

$$\text{Set: pull }S\to 0\Rightarrow Q=1,\bar Q=0;\quad \text{Reset: pull }R\to 0\Rightarrow Q=0,\bar Q=1$$

$$\text{S-R hold: as long as }S=R=1,\ (Q,\bar Q)\ \text{stays at }(1,0)\ \text{or }(0,1)$$

$$\text{D-Latch: Data }D\text{ + Enable }E\to (Q,\bar Q);\ \text{2 NAND gates added to S, R inputs of the SR latch}$$

$$\text{Two D-latches + 1 extra NAND between }E_{1}\text{ and }E_{2}\Rightarrow \text{clocked memory cell; chain of }E_{i}\Rightarrow \text{shift register}$$

$$|A_{OL}(\omega)|\sim\frac{1}{\omega RC}\quad\text{(open-loop gain rolls off above unity-gain frequency)}$$

$$\omega_{u}\sim\frac{1}{RC},\ \ f_{u}\sim 10^{5}\text{–}10^{6}\ \text{Hz}$$

$$\text{JFET (n-channel): }V=IR,\ \frac{dV}{dS}=R,\ \frac{dS}{dV}\sim\frac{1}{R};\ \text{negative }V_{gs}\text{ widens depletion region until pinch-off}$$

$$\text{JFET output: }I_{d}\text{ rises in the active/linear region, then saturates (}I_{d}\text{ independent of }V_{ds}\text{); curves shown for }V_{gs}=0,-1\,\text{V},-2\,\text{V},V_{gs}<-V_{p}$$

$$\text{D-MOSFET: insulating oxide allows }V_{gs}>0;\ \text{negative }V_{gs}\text{ depletes channel, positive }V_{gs}\text{ enhances it (extra carriers from p-region pulled to gate)}$$

$$\text{E-MOSFET (N-channel): off for }V_{gs}=0,\ \text{on for }V_{gs}>0\ \Rightarrow\ \text{single E-MOSFET + pull-up}=\text{NOT gate}$$

$$\text{P-mos FET / JFET convention: on for }V_{gs}=0,\ \text{off for }V_{gs}>0$$

$$\text{Bipolar NOT gate: }V_{in}=0\Rightarrow\text{off}\Rightarrow V_{out}\!\approx\!5\,\text{V};\ V_{in}=5\,\text{V}\Rightarrow\text{saturation (}V_{CE}\text{ small)}\Rightarrow V_{out}\!\approx\!0$$

$$\text{CMOS inverter: }V_{in}=+5\,\text{V}\Rightarrow\text{P-mos OFF, n-mos ON}\Rightarrow V_{out}\!=\!0;\ V_{in}=0\Rightarrow\text{P-mos ON, n-mos OFF}\Rightarrow V_{out}\!=\!+V_{S}$$

$$\text{n-mos: }V_{gs}=V_{d}-V_{g};\ g=1\text{ on, }g=0\text{ off}\quad\text{P-mos: }V_{gs}=V_{s}-V_{g};\ g=0\text{ on, }g=1\text{ off}$$

$$\text{No DC path from }+V_{S}\text{ to GND in either steady state }\Rightarrow\text{CMOS draws much less static power than a 1-transistor + 1-resistor NOT gate}$$

$$\text{Binary place value: }10101_{2}=1\!\cdot\!2^{4}+0\!\cdot\!2^{3}+1\!\cdot\!2^{2}+0\!\cdot\!2^{1}+1\!\cdot\!2^{0}=21_{10}$$

$$\text{Half-adder: }S=A\oplus B,\ C=A\!\cdot\!B,\ \text{so }A+B=CS\ \text{(C is the 2's place, S is the LSB)}$$

$$\text{Full-adder stage: inputs }(A,B,C_{in})\to(S,C_{out});\ S=A\oplus B\oplus C_{in},\ C_{out}=(A\!\cdot\!B)+\big((A\oplus B)\!\cdot\!C_{in}\big)$$

$$\text{4-bit ripple add: }A_{8}A_{4}A_{2}A_{1}+B_{8}B_{4}B_{2}B_{1}=C_{8,out}S_{8}S_{4}S_{2}S_{1}$$

$$\text{Gate/transistor budget: 1 four-bit adder}=4\text{ one-bit adders}=4\!\times\!5\text{ logic gates}=4\!\times\!5\!\times\!4\text{ transistors}=80\text{ transistors}$$

$$\text{Two 4-bit adders chained}\Rightarrow\text{adder for wider (8-bit) numbers — emergent computational complexity}$$

How to use this page
Read each displayed relation as solving one of seven jobs: (1) deciding whether the op-amp is in linear closed-loop mode (golden rule applies) or saturated/comparator mode (output rails to ±V_S); (2) placing positive feedback on the noninverting pin to make a Schmitt trigger or a relaxation oscillator (or recognising the same machinery inside the 555 IC); (3) mapping a saturated voltage onto a 0/1 logic level using the 0–3 V vs 3–5 V convention; (4) assembling those logic levels into combinational primitives (NOT, AND, NAND, OR, NOR, XOR) and the sequential SR / D-latch / shift-register family; (5) choosing the right transistor (JFET, D-MOSFET, E-MOSFET, P-mos) for a given biasing job by reading the I_d–V_gs/V_ds curves and the on/off conventions; (6) physically realising a logic gate at the transistor level (bipolar NOT in saturation, or the much lower-power CMOS pair, or a stacked CMOS pair for two-input gates); and (7) wiring those gates into binary arithmetic — half-adder, full-adder stage, then a ripple-carry 4-bit (and chainable 8-bit) adder. The chain "op-amp → comparator → digital level → gate → latch → register → adder" is the logic that ties the handwritten algebra together and motivates the closing remark about emergent computational complexity.
