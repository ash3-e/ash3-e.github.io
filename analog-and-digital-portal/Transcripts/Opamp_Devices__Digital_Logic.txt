Op-amp Devices and Digital Logic
Overview
Single-page lecture notes that cross from op-amp-based building blocks (open-loop comparator, Schmitt trigger with hysteresis, relaxation oscillator, 555-timer integrated circuit) into digital logic primitives (NOT, AND, NAND, OR, NOR, XOR with truth tables) and finishes on a bistable NAND-NAND flip-flop "memory." The page emphasises how op-amp gain rolls off above the unity-gain bandwidth (~10⁵–10⁶ Hz), why a Schmitt trigger reduces switching jitter on noisy signals, and how positive feedback turns the same comparator into a free-running RC oscillator. This transcript is a page-specific reconstruction for preview and search.

Problem coverage
The set is centered on signed-output op-amp circuits and on the elementary Boolean primitives. The page clearly includes the open-loop transfer V_out = A_OL(V_in⁺ − V_in⁻) with A_OL ≫ 1 and a roll-off frequency around the unity-gain point, the comparator output that latches to ±V_S as soon as V_in crosses zero, the Schmitt trigger with the symmetric hysteretic thresholds V_b = ±V_S R₁/(R₁+R₂) ≈ ±1 V using R₁ = 90 kΩ and R₂ = 10 kΩ, the relaxation oscillator built from the same Schmitt feedback wrapped around an R_F C charging branch (V_out = +V_S → cap charges → V_in⁻ crosses V_in⁺ → flip → cap discharges → flip back), the 555-timer block diagram with its 2/3 V_S and 1/3 V_S thresholds and its internal flip-flop with Q and Q̄ outputs, the digital-logic mapping 0 ↔ 0 V (range 0–3 V) and 1 ↔ +5 V (range 3–5 V), and full truth tables for NOT, AND, NAND, OR, NOR, XOR, plus the cross-coupled NAND flip-flop bistable memory whose state holds when both switches are open and is set or reset by closing S or R.

Key equations

$$V_{out}=A_{OL}(V_{in}^{+}-V_{in}^{-}),\qquad A_{OL}\gg 1$$

$$\text{Golden Rule (with feedback): }V_{in}^{+}=V_{in}^{-},\qquad I_{1}=I_{2}\quad\text{(Input draws no current)}$$

$$\text{Comparator: }V_{out}=\begin{cases}+V_{S} & V_{in}^{+}>V_{in}^{-}\\ -V_{S} & V_{in}^{+}<V_{in}^{-}\end{cases}$$

$$V_{b}=\pm V_{S}\,\frac{R_{1}}{R_{1}+R_{2}}=\pm 1\ \text{V}\ \text{(Schmitt thresholds, }R_{1}=90\,\text{k}\Omega,R_{2}=10\,\text{k}\Omega,V_{S}=1.0\ \text{V)}$$

$$\text{Relaxation oscillator: }V_{C}(t)=V_{S}+(V_{C}(0)-V_{S})\,e^{-t/\tau},\ \ \tau=R_{F}C$$

$$T_{osc}=2R_{F}C\,\ln\!\left[\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}\right],\quad \beta=\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}+R_{2}}$$

$$\text{555 thresholds: }\tfrac{2}{3}V_{S}\ \text{(upper)},\ \tfrac{1}{3}V_{S}\ \text{(lower)}$$

$$\text{Logic levels: }0\leftrightarrow 0\text{–}3\ \text{V},\ \ 1\leftrightarrow 3\text{–}5\ \text{V}$$

$$\text{NOT}(A)=\bar A:\ 0\!\to\!1,\ 1\!\to\!0$$

$$\text{AND}(A,B)=A\!\cdot\!B:\ (0,0)\!\to\!0,\ (1,0)\!\to\!0,\ (0,1)\!\to\!0,\ (1,1)\!\to\!1$$

$$\text{NAND}(A,B)=\overline{A\!\cdot\!B}:\ (0,0)\!\to\!1,\ (1,0)\!\to\!1,\ (0,1)\!\to\!1,\ (1,1)\!\to\!0$$

$$\text{OR}(A,B)=A+B:\ (0,0)\!\to\!0,\ (1,0)\!\to\!1,\ (0,1)\!\to\!1,\ (1,1)\!\to\!1$$

$$\text{NOR}(A,B)=\overline{A+B}:\ (0,0)\!\to\!1,\ (1,0)\!\to\!0,\ (0,1)\!\to\!0,\ (1,1)\!\to\!0$$

$$\text{XOR}(A,B)=A\oplus B:\ (0,0)\!\to\!0,\ (1,0)\!\to\!1,\ (0,1)\!\to\!1,\ (1,1)\!\to\!0$$

$$\text{NAND flip-flop holds state when both inputs are HIGH; }Q\ \text{and }\bar Q\ \text{are complementary}$$

$$\text{Set: pull }S\to 0\Rightarrow Q=1,\bar Q=0$$

$$\text{Reset: pull }R\to 0\Rightarrow Q=0,\bar Q=1$$

$$|A_{OL}(\omega)|\sim\frac{1}{\omega RC}\quad\text{(open-loop gain rolls off above unity-gain frequency)}$$

$$\omega_{u}\sim\frac{1}{RC},\ \ f_{u}\sim 10^{5}\text{–}10^{6}\ \text{Hz}$$

$$V_{in}=\pm V_{S}\Rightarrow\ \text{output toggles between rails (saturation)}$$

How to use this page
Read each displayed relation as solving one of four jobs: deciding whether the op-amp is in linear closed-loop mode (golden rule applies) or saturated/comparator mode (output rails to ±V_S), placing positive feedback on the noninverting pin to make a Schmitt or relaxation oscillator, mapping the result onto a 0/1 logic level, or assembling those logic levels into combinational and sequential primitives (gates and flip-flops). That is the logic tying the handwritten algebra together.
