Analog and Digital Conversion
Overview
Single-page lecture notes covering analog-to-digital and digital-to-analog conversion, the data converters that implement them, and the digital signal processing concepts (Nyquist sampling, FPGA implementation, feedback control) that surround them, finishing with thermal Johnson-Nyquist noise as the physical limit on resolution. The page works through 1-bit comparators, multi-bit flash A/D, summing-amplifier DAC, successive approximation register (SAR), sigma-delta and ramp converters, oversampling, then crosses into DSP architecture and noise measurement. Key equations

$$V_{in}\in[-V_{max},+V_{max}]\ \to\ \{0,1,\ldots,2^{N}-1\}\quad\text{(N-bit ADC)}$$

$$\text{LSB}=\frac{2V_{max}}{2^{N}}=\frac{V_{max}}{2^{N-1}}$$

$$\text{Example: }\pm 1\ \text{V},\ N=4\Rightarrow \text{LSB}=\frac{2}{16}=0.125\ \text{V}$$

$$\text{Example: }\pm 10\ \text{V},\ N=16\Rightarrow \text{LSB}\approx 300\ \mu\text{V}$$

$$\text{Comparator: }V_{out}=\begin{cases}1 & V_{in}>0\\ 0 & V_{in}<0\end{cases}$$

$$\text{Flash A/D thresholds: }\pm V_{max},\ \pm V_{max}/2$$

$$V_{out}^{\text{DAC}}=\frac{R}{2R}A_{3}+\frac{R}{4R}A_{2}+\frac{R}{8R}A_{1}+\frac{R}{16R}A_{0}=\tfrac{1}{2}A_{3}+\tfrac{1}{4}A_{2}+\tfrac{1}{8}A_{1}+\tfrac{1}{16}A_{0}$$

$$\text{SAR: starting guess }1000=V_{max}/2\ \text{then refine bit by bit}$$

$$\text{If }V_{in}<\frac{V_{max}}{2}\Rightarrow Q_{i}=0;\ \text{if }V_{in}>\frac{V_{max}}{2}\Rightarrow Q_{i}=1$$

$$f_{s}\ge 2 f_{\text{signal}}\quad\text{(Nyquist sampling theorem)}$$

$$\text{Oversampling: }4^{n}\ \text{averages}\Rightarrow n\ \text{extra bits of resolution}$$

$$y_{n+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}y_{i}\quad\text{(general digital filter)}$$

$$\langle V_{n}^{2}\rangle=4k_{B}T\,R\,\Delta f\quad\text{(Johnson–Nyquist thermal noise)}$$

$$k_{B}=1.38\!\times\!10^{-23}\ \text{J/K},\ T=300\ \text{K}$$

$$R=1\ \text{k}\Omega,\ \Delta f=1\ \text{MHz}\Rightarrow\sqrt{\langle V_{n}^{2}\rangle}=4\!\times\!10^{-6}=4\ \mu\text{V}$$

$$R=10^{6}\ \Omega,\ \Delta f=100\ \text{MHz}\Rightarrow\sqrt{\langle V_{n}^{2}\rangle}\approx 1\ \text{mV}$$

$$\omega_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}\quad\text{(LRC harmonic-oscillator analog)}$$

$$\omega_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{LC}}\quad\text{(LRC circuit resonance)}$$

$$F(t)=-kx(t)-m\Gamma\frac{dx}{dt}+F_{\text{test}}+F_{\text{feedback}}$$

$$F_{\text{feedback}}=-k_{p}x(t)-k_{D}\frac{dx}{dt}\Rightarrow F(t)=-(k+k_{p})x(t)-(m\Gamma+k_{D})\frac{dx}{dt}+F_{\text{test}}$$

$$\text{Sigma-delta: vary pulse width + pulse rate near 50}\%\ \text{duty}$$

How to use this page
Read each displayed relation as solving one of four jobs: mapping analog amplitudes onto N digital bins (LSB, range, dynamic range), implementing that mapping with comparators / summing amplifiers / a SAR feedback loop, choosing the sampling rate from Nyquist and improving resolution by oversampling, or computing the noise floor that ultimately limits how fine the bins can be. That is the logic tying the handwritten algebra together.
