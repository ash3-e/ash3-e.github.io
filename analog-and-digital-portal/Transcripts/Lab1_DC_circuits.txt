Lab 1: DC Circuits
Overview
First lab of the semester, introducing the breadboard, the digital multimeter (DMM), Ohm's law, and the two basic resistor networks: voltage and current dividers, with a Thevenin reduction at the end. The student measures actual resistor values (R_1 ≈ 9.882 kΩ, R_2 ≈ 19.847 kΩ) and the actual supply voltage (V ≈ 5.0426 V), computes V_ba and V_cb across each leg of a 5 V divider, takes Ohm's-law currents, computes total dissipated power and the percentage in R_2, then loads the divider with R_L = 10 kΩ and 20 kΩ to see how the loading drags the open-circuit V_ba down toward V_th. The lab ends with a parallel three-resistor "current divider" where the per-branch currents add to the supply current and the per-branch powers add to the total. Problem coverage
The set is centered on hand-measuring DC quantities and confirming that the measurements obey the textbook formulae. The pages clearly include the breadboard tour (PB-503 with built-in ±5–15 V supplies, power-bus rows, core-area columns, bus strips), the DMM modes (V, I, R, diode test) and how meter loading can perturb a high-impedance measurement, the simple two-resistor voltage divider where V_ba = V·R_2/(R_1 + R_2) is checked against the DMM read-out, the Ohm's-law re-check V_ba = IR_2 confirming the same number, the total-power calculation P_total = V²/R_eq = VI = I²R, the percentage of the total power dissipated in R_2 (~67 %), the safe-current/safe-voltage exercise for a 2 kΩ resistor rated at ¼ W (I_max = √(P/R), V_max = √(PR)), the loaded divider with R_L = 10 kΩ giving the Thevenin equivalent V_th = V·R_2/(R_1+R_2) and R_th = R_1∥R_2 ≈ 6.62 kΩ so V_ba(loaded) drops to about 2.47 V, the same exercise repeated with R_L = 20 kΩ where the equivalent parallel becomes R_eq ≈ 9.92 kΩ and V_ba ≈ 2.53 V, and finally the three-resistor parallel "current divider" with R_1 ≈ 9.882 kΩ, R_2 ≈ 0.987 kΩ, R_3 ≈ 4.676 kΩ where the branch currents I_1 ≈ 0.4529 mA, I_2 ≈ 0.374 mA, I_3 ≈ 0.078 mA give per-branch powers 2.026 mW, 0.1381 mW, 0.0284 mW summing to the total.

Key equations

$$V=IR\quad\text{(Ohm's law)}$$

$$P=\frac{V^{2}}{R}=VI=I^{2}R$$

$$V_{ba}=V\cdot\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}+R_{2}}\quad\text{(voltage divider)}$$

$$V_{cb}=V\cdot\frac{R_{1}}{R_{1}+R_{2}}\quad\text{(voltage on the other leg)}$$

$$I=\frac{V}{R_{1}+R_{2}}\quad\text{(series-loop current)}$$

$$V_{ba}=I\,R_{2}\quad\text{(Ohm's-law cross-check)}$$

$$P_{total}=V\cdot I=\frac{V^{2}}{R_{1}+R_{2}}\quad\text{(power from supply)}$$

$$\%P_{R_{2}}=\frac{P_{R_{2}}}{P_{total}}\times 100\%=\frac{I^{2}R_{2}}{I^{2}(R_{1}+R_{2})}\times 100\%=\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}+R_{2}}\times 100\%$$

$$I_{max}=\sqrt{\frac{P_{rated}}{R}}\quad\text{(safe current for rated power)}$$

$$V_{max}=\sqrt{P_{rated}\,R}\quad\text{(safe voltage for rated power)}$$

$$\text{Thevenin: }V_{th}=V\cdot\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}+R_{2}},\quad R_{th}=\frac{R_{1}R_{2}}{R_{1}+R_{2}}$$

$$V_{ba,\text{loaded}}=V_{th}\cdot\frac{R_{L}}{R_{th}+R_{L}}\quad\text{(loaded divider output)}$$

$$R_{eq}=\frac{R_{2}\,R_{L}}{R_{2}+R_{L}}\quad\text{(parallel combination of }R_{2}\text{ and }R_{L}\text{)}$$

$$V_{ba}=V\cdot\frac{R_{eq}}{R_{1}+R_{eq}}\quad\text{(divider with parallel load)}$$

$$\frac{1}{R_{\|}}=\frac{1}{R_{1}}+\frac{1}{R_{2}}+\frac{1}{R_{3}}\quad\text{(parallel resistors)}$$

$$I_{i}=\frac{V}{R_{i}}\quad\text{(branch current of parallel divider)}$$

$$I_{total}=\sum_{i}I_{i}\quad\text{(KCL: sum of branch currents)}$$

$$P_{i}=I_{i}^{2}R_{i}=\frac{V^{2}}{R_{i}}\quad\text{(per-branch power)}$$

$$P_{total}=\sum_{i}P_{i}\quad\text{(total dissipation = sum of branch dissipations)}$$

$$V_{ba}=I R_{2}=(1.696\!\times\!10^{-4}\,\text{A})(19.847\,\text{k}\Omega)\approx 3.35\ \text{V}\quad\text{(measured)}$$

$$P_{total}=5.0426\cdot 1.696\!\times\!10^{-4}=8.55\!\times\!10^{-4}\ \text{W}\approx 0.855\ \text{mW}$$

How to use this page
Read each displayed relation as solving one of four jobs: applying Ohm's law to a single branch, dividing voltage between two series resistors, replacing a sub-network by its Thevenin equivalent before connecting a load, or summing per-branch currents and powers in a parallel divider. That is the logic tying the handwritten algebra together.
