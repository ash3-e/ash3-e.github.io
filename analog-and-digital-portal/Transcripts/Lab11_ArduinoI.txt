Lab 11: Arduino I (Digital and Analog I/O)
Overview
First Arduino lab: an introduction to the Arduino UNO (ATmega328 microcontroller, 14 digital I/O pins, 6 analog input pins, 6 PWM-capable digital outputs marked with ~, USB programming, 16 MHz clock, 32 KB flash, 2 KB SRAM, 1 KB EEPROM) and the Arduino IDE (sketches in C/C++ with setup() and loop() functions). The student blinks an LED with digital output, reads a digital input from a button while exploring why a floating input is unpredictable (motivating pull-up and pull-down resistors), reads an analog voltage from a potentiometer through the Arduino's 10-bit ADC (1024 levels over the 0–5 V range giving a 4.88 mV LSB), changes AREF to 1.1 V to gain finer resolution over a small range, and finally drives an LED's brightness with a PWM "analog" output at the Arduino's ~490 Hz PWM frequency. This transcript is a page-specific reconstruction for preview and search.

Problem coverage
The set is centered on first-pass digital and analog I/O with a microcontroller. The pages clearly include the UNO board tour (USB port, power socket, reset pin, 3.3 V and 5 V power pins, GND, digital pins 2–13 with ~ marking PWM-capable, analog input A0–A5 read via 10-bit ADC, RX/TX on pins 0 and 1 for serial), the IDE basics (semicolons end statements, curly braces for blocks, // and /* */ for comments, identifiers must be defined before use), the Blink sketch and oscilloscope confirmation that the high-time and low-time match the delay() values, the LED current measurement V_R/R = (3.3 V − V_LED)/330 Ω ≈ 4 mA, the 50 Hz threshold above which the LED appears continuously lit (related to persistence of vision and to the 60 Hz / 50 Hz mains in the US / Europe), the floating-input lesson (jumper wire on Pin 2 picks up 60 Hz hum from the hand) showing why pull-up (5 V → resistor → input → switch → GND) and pull-down (GND → resistor → input → switch → 5 V) configurations are mandatory, the potentiometer + AnalogInput sketch with Serial.println(sensorValue) reading 0 at 0 V and 1023 at 4.92 V (resolution = 5 V / 1024 = 4.88 mV ≈ 0.005 V/count), the AREF switch to 1.1 V giving 1.07 mV/count and confirming better resolution over a smaller range, the Fade sketch that drives Pin 9 with analogWrite(0–255) producing a 490 Hz PWM whose duty cycle controls average voltage and therefore LED brightness, and the appendix specifications (operating voltage 5 V, input voltage 7–12 V recommended, 40 mA max per I/O pin, 200 mA max V_CC).

Key equations

$$V_{LED}=V_{src}-IR\Rightarrow I_{LED}=\frac{V_{R_{330}}}{330\ \Omega}$$

$$\text{LED current example: }I=\frac{3.30\ \text{V}-2.80\ \text{V}}{330\ \Omega}\approx 4\ \text{mA}$$

$$\text{ADC resolution: }N_{levels}=2^{N_{bits}}=2^{10}=1024$$

$$\text{Default LSB: }\frac{V_{ref}}{2^{N}}=\frac{5\ \text{V}}{1024}\approx 4.88\ \text{mV}$$

$$\text{Internal AREF: }\frac{1.1\ \text{V}}{1024}\approx 1.07\ \text{mV (5× finer)}$$

$$V_{in}=\frac{\text{analogRead}()}{1023}\cdot V_{ref}\quad\text{(linear ADC mapping)}$$

$$\text{analogRead}(0\ \text{V})=0,\quad \text{analogRead}(V_{ref})=1023$$

$$\text{Pull-up: }V_{out}=\begin{cases}V_{cc} & \text{switch open}\\ 0 & \text{switch closed}\end{cases}$$

$$\text{Pull-down: }V_{out}=\begin{cases}0 & \text{switch open}\\ V_{cc} & \text{switch closed}\end{cases}$$

$$\text{Pull-resistor value: }R_{pull}\sim 1\text{–}10\ \text{k}\Omega$$

$$\text{PWM duty cycle: }D=\frac{T_{high}}{T_{period}}\in[0,1]$$

$$\text{analogWrite}(\text{value})\text{: value}\in[0,255]\Rightarrow D=\frac{\text{value}}{255}$$

$$\bar V_{out}=D\cdot V_{cc}\quad\text{(average PWM voltage seen by slow load)}$$

$$f_{PWM}\approx 490\ \text{Hz (Arduino UNO default)}$$

$$T_{PWM}=\frac{1}{490}\approx 2.04\ \text{ms (period of one PWM cycle)}$$

$$\text{Map: }y=\text{map}(x,0,1023,0,255)\Rightarrow y=\frac{255\,x}{1023}$$

$$\text{Persistence of vision: }f_{flicker}\gtrsim 50\ \text{Hz}\Rightarrow \text{LED appears continuous}$$

$$f_{mains}=60\ \text{Hz (US)}=50\ \text{Hz (Europe)}\quad\text{(reason for 50 Hz threshold)}$$

$$\text{Max source/sink per I/O pin: }I_{pin}\le 40\ \text{mA}$$

$$\text{Max current per V_{CC}/GND: }I\le 200\ \text{mA}$$

$$\text{Clock speed: }f_{CPU}=16\ \text{MHz},\quad \text{flash} = 32\ \text{KB},\quad \text{SRAM}=2\ \text{KB}$$

How to use this page
Read each displayed relation as solving one of four jobs: converting a measured resistor voltage drop into a current through Ohm's law, mapping a 10-bit ADC count back to volts via V = (count/1023)·V_ref, choosing a pull-up or pull-down topology to give a digital input a defined level, or computing the average voltage seen by a slow load when an Arduino PWM output drives it at duty D. That is the logic tying the handwritten algebra together.
