Particle Physics
Overview
This note is a broad final-topic map: particle physics, detector ideas, force carriers, hadrons and leptons, spontaneous symmetry breaking, the Higgs mechanism, general relativity, cosmology, and the cosmic microwave background. It reads like a one-page bridge from microscopic particle structure to large-scale spacetime geometry.

Three branches
The sheet opens by placing particle physics between related areas:
solid-state and condensed-matter physics, particle physics, and astronomy/cosmology. The organizing idea is that small-scale fields and particles connect to both material behavior and cosmological evolution.

Cloud chamber and magnetic tracks
The cloud-chamber sketch shows radioactive material inside a magnetic field with aerosolized water beads. Charged particles leave visible tracks because they ionize the vapor as they move. The force on a moving charge is written as
$$\mathbf{F}=q\,\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}.$$
The magnetic force bends the path, so circular motion is estimated by equating centripetal and magnetic force:
$$\frac{mv^2}{r}=qvB.$$
If a particle is accelerated through a potential difference,
$$q\Delta V=\frac12 mv^2,$$
then a mass-to-charge relation can be inferred from the radius:
$$r^2=\left(\frac{m}{q}\right)\frac{2\Delta V}{B^2}.$$
The note labels this as a way to identify different substances or particles from trajectory shape.

Nuclear scale and the strong force
The page asks what keeps protons together in the nucleus. It compares the electromagnetic repulsion scale
$$U\sim \frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r},$$
with a typical nuclear distance
$$r\sim 10^{-15}\text{ m}=10^{-13}\text{ cm},$$
giving energies on the MeV scale rather than ordinary thermal energies like
$$k_B T\sim 25\text{ meV}.$$
The conclusion is that the strong force cannot be replaced by ordinary electromagnetic binding. The strong interaction is represented by bosonic fields and gluons, while Feynman diagrams are used as the bookkeeping language for particle interactions.

Quarks, leptons, bosons, and hadrons
The Standard Model table marks three generations of fermions. Quarks are listed as
$$u,d;\qquad c,s;\qquad t,b,$$
with charges $+2/3$ and $-1/3$. Leptons are listed as the electron, muon, tau, and their neutrinos. The bosons listed include gluons for the strong nuclear interaction, photons for electromagnetism, and $W^\pm$ and $Z^0$ bosons for the weak nuclear interaction.

Hadrons are anything made of quarks. The note separates mesons and baryons:
mesons are quark-antiquark pairs, while baryons contain three quarks. Nucleons are described as composite particles, like atoms are composite at a larger scale. Color charge is summarized as red, green, and blue combining to a neutral white state, with anticolor-color pairs also forming neutral combinations.

Fine tuning and force ratios
The sheet flags fine tuning through unitless ratios of physical constants. One example is the ratio of gravitational to electric force:
$$\frac{F_G}{F_E}=\frac{Gm_e^2}{ke^2}\sim 10^{-45}.$$
The note frames this as one of the striking numerical hierarchies in physics and connects it to anthropic-principle language.

Spontaneous symmetry breaking and Higgs physics
The spontaneous-symmetry-breaking sketch shows a symmetric high-temperature state and a lower-temperature state with stable oscillation modes in the potential well. The potential is sketched in the form of an order-parameter field, with a high-temperature minimum at the origin and lower-temperature minima away from it. The note labels the Higgs field, Higgs boson, and Goldstone boson. The core idea is that quantized oscillations of the field around a chosen vacuum state appear as particles.

General relativity
The general-relativity side of the sheet connects special relativity and gravity through the equivalence principle:
an accelerated reference frame can locally resemble a gravitational field. Gravity is marked as acceleration,
$$a=g.$$
The page then describes four-dimensional geodesics. What appears to an observer as a force or curved path can be read geometrically as motion along a shortest path in curved spacetime. The relativistic energy relation appears as
$$E^2=(mc^2)^2+(pc)^2.$$
The cosmological constant is shown as a free parameter,
$$\Lambda,$$
with the note that setting or changing it alters the universe's expansion behavior.

Cosmology and the microwave background
Cosmology is organized around redshift, Hubble-style expansion, and the sign or size of the cosmological constant. The Doppler-shift sketch describes velocities of objects relative to us. The Hubble plot points to Lemaitre, a closed-universe picture, and recent data favoring an open or expanding universe. The sheet lists possible futures: Big Crunch, Big Rip or Freeze, and heat death, depending on matter density and the cosmological constant.

The cosmic microwave background is described as the decoupling of the electromagnetic field from quark or matter fields after the universe became cool enough for photons to travel freely. The note marks the present-day temperature as approximately
$$T\approx 3\text{ K},$$
and sketches a photon Planck distribution.

Anthropic-principle notes
The bottom of the page lists weak, strong, and final anthropic-principle ideas. The weak version says we should expect to find ourselves in a universe compatible with our existence. The stronger versions are presented more philosophically, including Boltzmann-brain language and comments about deism or theism. These are recorded as part of the note's final conceptual map, not as a derivation.
